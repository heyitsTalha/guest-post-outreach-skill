# Pitch guidelines

**Subject:** specific, e.g. `Guest post idea for <Site>: <topic>`

**Body (plain text, 90–150 words):**

1. Greet the editor by name if the guidelines page gives one, otherwise "Hi <Site> team".
2. One real, specific detail from their site or guidelines (a recent post, a listed topic, their word count). No generic flattery.
3. Who you are, honestly: `<sender_name>`, `<sender_role>`, one line about the company.
4. 2–3 article titles that fit **their** listed topics (use `pitch_ideas` as raw material, adapt to the site).
5. Promise: original, unpublished, follows their word count and style; one relevant link only if their guidelines allow.
6. Short, low-pressure close: "Happy to send an outline if any of these fit."

**Never:** a link in the pitch, an offer of payment, "SEO" or "backlink" language, hard selling, invented credentials, AI-sounding filler.

## Example

```
Subject: Guest post idea for Example Safety: making risk assessments stick

Hi Sam,

I read your recent piece on near-miss reporting and noticed your guidelines ask for practical, field-tested advice of 800+ words.

I'm Jordan, part of the team at Example Ltd, a safety training provider. I'd like to contribute one of these:

1. Five risk assessment mistakes supervisors make on busy sites
2. How to turn toolbox talks into real behaviour change
3. A simple monthly safety audit checklist for small contractors

The article would be original, follow your word count, and include one relevant link only if your guidelines allow it.

Happy to send an outline if any of these fit.

Best,
Jordan
```
